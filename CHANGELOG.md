# Changelog

Each entry says what changed and the observed failure that prompted it. Newest first.

## 2026-09-11

The two lists were shown and confirmed as proposed, and the briefs came out as before: the schema brief carried the design again, and the GPU brief carried the runtime-tuning verdict for a third time plus noise the confirmed sort had been expected to remove (a retired model, a removed node, a control-plane plan the brief itself called "not this question", a start script that is not what runs). Two flat lists invite nodding along; step 1 now proposes a placement per numbered item with a one-line reason, and the user answers by number, the same mechanism as a grilling round. The relevance bar returns as a positive ("a fact travels when the question turns on it"), since removing it on the theory that the gate covers it was wrong.
  - Run 7 was the first downstream test: a brief on an AI-bot analytics SaaS pasted into a fresh conversation. The answer corrected two givens, added facts, refused the offered wedge, proposed a different metric set and advised against the standalone product. Fresh eyes worked where the question had a fact base and an undecided verdict. Two of its added facts were verified: BotBase for Operators shipped 28 August 2026; the 15 September default block on Training and Agent crawlers is real but applies to ad-bearing pages on new domains, new customers and existing Free-tier customers, not "all customers" as the answer claimed.
  - The same brief said "don't just default to what a prior conversation concluded", and the answer echoed it. Third run to reference the withheld conversation in the ask (runs 3 and 5 before it), so step 2 gains the positive form: the brief reads as the first time the question has been asked.
  - Runs 2, 3 and 5 all confirmed the schema design as requirements, which means for that topic a review is what the user wants. Step 2 gains the branch: if the design stays on the travels list, the brief includes it and asks for a critique, instead of hedging between "a schema, or a critique of one" the reader cannot see.

## 2026-09-10

- **Redesign `fresh-eyes` around a visible sort and the requirements-versus-design pair, after runs 3 and 4.** Rerunning both conversations on the sharpened version reproduced the failures: the schema brief still filed the design under "What I already know" (tagged responsibility bullets, a seniority ladder, compensation as min, max, currency and period) and closed with "not just a critique of a structure I haven't shown you"; the GPU brief carried the same verdict as run 1, reworded to "I consider the runtime-level tuning largely done", dropped the context-growth-per-turn fact, and the model asked on its own how to treat "two vLLM facts I surfaced". The two schema briefs also disagreed on whether the stated-versus-inferred flag was in or out.
  - Six further patches were drafted (a rejection test for constraints, a setup-versus-implication rule, a final-position rule, routing exclusions out of the givens, restoring "the brief refers to nothing it withholds", and an open-point check in the done-when) and rejected as a set. They would have taken step 1 to eleven stacked rules, six of them prohibitions, after two rounds of sharpening had not moved the failure: sediment, the elephant, and a leading word (*fresh eyes*) too weak to carry the withholding rule on its own.
  - The change instead: the body sorts on **requirements versus design**, a pretrained pair that already knows a requirements document does not contain the design, replacing the case/first-opinion vocabulary and the constraint tests. Step 1 shows the two lists, **travels** and **stays**, and the user confirms them before the brief is written: what counts as a requirement is the user's decision, and every failure so far was a sort the user would have corrected in one glance. The model's ad hoc question in run 4 becomes the designed checkpoint. Three short clauses survive from the patches because runs showed each: setup travels and its implication stays, the final position is the one sorted, an open point travels as a question. The relevance bar, the foreclosure check and the narrowing rule are gone; a confirmed sort covers them. Template placeholders no longer repeat the step rules. 630 words to about 430.
- **Sharpen `fresh-eyes` after its first two runs.** Both briefs looked right and both carried the first opinion through.
  - Run 1 (a GPU serving capacity question) passed one conclusion as a given, "most runtime-side optimization we know of has already been done", a sentence that tells the reader where not to look without giving them anything to check; carried givens that did not bear on the question (a retired model, a control-plane migration plan); and said nothing about the load, latency and token numbers the question turns on, so the reader's first move would have been to ask for them.
  - Run 2 (a schema design question) filed the design itself under "What I already know": deduplicated duty lists with cross-references, stable identifiers, seniority and management as two fields, controlled versus approximate taxonomy. The question asked for the whole shape while the givens fixed it. History words ("no longer want", "considered this and ruled it out", "prototyping in TypeScript") narrated the first conversation. The one open point appeared three times. A given pushed enforcement upstream while the ask demanded the schema handle it.
  - The change: step 1 gets a checkable test (a constraint bounds the question and the reader can check it; a conclusion answers it or says where not to look; a constraint carries the need it serves, never the reasoning that chose it), a relevance bar, and the rule that constraints which answer the question mean the question must narrow. Step 2 states givens in the present tense with no account of how they came to hold, one mention per point with the open point in the ask alone, unknown facts stated as unknown among the givens, and a done-when that no given forecloses the ask. The "ask when a point could be either" line is gone: the feared over-asking never happened, the opposite did, and a sharper bound replaces it. A separate "What I don't know" section was considered and rejected: it is empty for any question that does not turn on measurements, and an empty section is padding.
- **Rename `second-opinion` to `fresh-eyes`.** Prompted by choosing the name, not by a run. Both words are pretrained: *second opinion* carries the withholding rule for free (the second doctor gets the records, not the diagnosis) but presumes doubt about the first answer; *fresh eyes* names the reader's state, which is the same move as `wait-what` naming the listener's state, and matches the words in the user's head at the trigger moment ("I want a fresh view on this"). What *fresh eyes* does not carry is the withholding rule, since the idiom usually means looking at the existing draft, so the body now states it in one line: fresh eyes on the question, never on the answer. Rejected: `clean-room` (strongest on withholding, but drags in reimplementation and two-team furniture), `referral` (referral letters carry the provisional diagnosis), `devil's-advocate` (opposition, not independence).
- **Drop the leading word from the brief's title.** The template titled the output "Second opinion: topic", which told the fresh conversation a first answer existed and invited it to guess at or hedge against it, the exact anchoring the skill exists to avoid. The title is now the topic alone; the leading word belongs to the agent writing the brief, not to its reader.
- **Add `second-opinion`** (productivity, user-invoked). Prompted by a recurring pain: a deep-dive conversation narrows a topic, and getting a fresh view from a new conversation meant re-typing the whole framing by hand. The brief it writes carries the narrowed question and the constraints, and withholds every conclusion this conversation reached, because a reader who can see the first answer argues with it instead of forming their own. Not yet run on a real conversation; the bound most likely to need sharpening is step 1's "ask when a point could be either a constraint or a conclusion", which may ask too often.
